# Orbit Platform — Performance Readout

*Source: sitespeed.io measurement captured 22 October 2026. It covers 19 page loads across three user roles (Admin, Airport Manager, Analyst) on the Orbit aviation surveillance platform. The Admin numbers come from the live site (measured 18 September); the Airport Manager and Analyst numbers come from the pre-production site. Categories used throughout: **fast** = under 2.5s, **acceptable** = 2.5–4s, **slow** = over 4s.*

## Executive summary

- **Orbit is usable but consistently sluggish: none of the 19 measured pages reached "fast," 13 of 19 are "slow" (over 4 seconds), and the average page takes about 5.2 seconds to fully load.** Only the reference-data screens (POE Master, Campaign Master, User Management) land in the acceptable band at roughly 3.6–3.9 seconds.
- **The single biggest problem is the Live Arrivals monitor.** It is the slowest experience in every role: 8.6 seconds for an Analyst, 8.4 seconds for an Airport Manager, and 6.8 seconds for an Admin. It also fires **470 to 526 separate data requests** per page and moves around **1 megabyte of traffic**, which is what pushes the wait to nearly nine seconds.
- **Region Master is the second worst offender at 6.8–6.9 seconds**, again because it issues **267 to 277 separate requests** — the screen asks the backend for country-by-country airport data one piece at a time instead of in one go.
- **Every single page pays a fixed tax of roughly 1.3 seconds before anything appears**, plus another 0.6–0.75 seconds for a shared layout file that is fetched twice. This overhead is present on all 19 page loads, including the fast ones, and is the reason even simple screens such as User Management cannot get under 4 seconds.
- **Business risk:** slow is not the same as broken, but the heaviest screens are the ones used on a live operational floor, where a monitor that takes 7–9 seconds to settle is a monitor people stop trusting and start cross-checking against other systems.

## Role by role

**Admin (live site)**

- Overall this feels like a working platform with a lot of waiting attached. The Admin screens average **about 4.9 seconds** to fully load, and 5 of the 7 pages are **slow**. The fastest Admin screen is **User Management at roughly 3.6 seconds (acceptable)**, making 27 data requests, followed by POE Master at **3.8 seconds (acceptable)** with 32 requests. The slowest is **Region Master at about 6.9 seconds (slow)** with 267 requests, with **Live Arrivals close behind at 6.8 seconds (slow)** and a staggering **513 data requests**. The middle ground is the Airport Monitor at **4.3 seconds (slow)** and JFK Operational at **4.5 seconds (slow)**, both of which are content-rich but reasonably disciplined at 21–22 requests.
- What this means for an administrator: managing users and reference data feels tolerably quick and can be done in passing, but anything involving regions or live arrivals demands a pause of six to seven seconds, and the Live Arrivals screen behaves differently depending on the role and environment it is opened from.

**Airport Manager (pre-production)**

- This is the role closest to the frontline, and it is also the slowest role overall at an average of **about 5.7 seconds per page**. The best experience is **POE Master at roughly 3.8 seconds (acceptable)**, which is efficient at only 23 requests. The worst is **JFK Live Arrivals at about 8.4 seconds (slow)**, firing **470 requests** and moving roughly 950 KB, with **IAD Live Arrivals at 7.4 seconds (slow)** and **270 requests** close behind. The two operational screens, **IAD Operational Command at 4.3 seconds (slow)** and **JFK Airport Monitor Operational at 4.5 seconds (slow)**, are the well-behaved ones at just 14 requests each.
- What this means for an airport manager: the simple operational views are already efficient and load a sensible amount of data. The live-arrivals views — the ones used to answer "what is happening right now?" — are where the waiting lands, and they are waiting on many small calls arriving in a crowd rather than on one big one.

**Analyst (pre-production)**

- The Analyst experience sits in the middle: an average of **about 5.1 seconds**, with 4 of 7 pages **slow** and 3 **acceptable**. The quickest is **User Management at roughly 3.7 seconds (acceptable)** with 27 requests, closely followed by POE Master at **3.8 seconds (acceptable)** and Campaign Master at **3.9 seconds (acceptable)**. The pain point is **JFK Live Arrivals at about 8.6 seconds (slow)** — the slowest measurement in the whole report — which fires **526 data requests** and moves around 1.3 MB, with **Region Master at 6.8 seconds (slow)** and **277 requests** also straining.
- What this means for an analyst: the desk-work screens (reference data, campaigns, user administration) are fine to work in all day. The exploration screens (live arrivals and region master) are where an analyst is likely to open a second browser tab or work around the product, because they are the screens where the wait is longest and most repeated.

## Pages that need attention

- **JFK Live Arrivals — Analyst (8.6 seconds, 526 requests) and Airport Manager (8.4 seconds, 470 requests).** Why it is slow: the screen pulls country-by-country airport data as hundreds of individual calls; the five slowest of them each take 3.8–3.9 seconds, and the browser uploads about 1 MB while waiting. Suggestion: **serve the whole live-arrivals picture in one consolidated feed instead of several hundred country lookups**, so the screen arrives in one round trip rather than a crowd of them.
- **Region Master — Admin (6.9 seconds, 267 requests) and Analyst (6.8 seconds, 277 requests).** Why it is slow: the same one-country-at-a-time pattern, with the slowest country lookups taking 1.2–2.5 seconds each and roughly 830–930 KB exchanged. Suggestion: **load the list of regions first and fetch a country's details only when the user opens it**, so the screen shows structure in under 3 seconds and detail on demand.
- **IAD Live Arrivals — Airport Manager (7.4 seconds, 270 requests).** Why it is slow: identical to JFK Live Arrivals, and slightly lighter, but still slow enough to break the flow of a live operational check. Suggestion: **fold it into the same consolidated feed fix** as JFK Live Arrivals so both airports benefit from one change.
- **The shared "every page" overhead — 19 pages, roughly 1.3 seconds of server wait plus a duplicated 51 KB layout file taking 0.6–0.75 seconds.** Why it is slow: every page, including the fast ones, waits around 1.2–1.5 seconds for the page itself to come back from the server, then downloads the same layout file twice. Suggestion: **stop downloading the shared layout file twice and serve the page shell faster**, which lifts every screen on the platform at once.
- **JFK Airport Monitor Operational — Analyst (4.8 seconds), JFK Operational — Admin (4.5 seconds), Campaign Master — Admin (4.4 seconds), Airport Monitor — Admin (4.3 seconds) and Analyst (4.3 seconds), IAD Operational — Airport Manager (4.3 seconds).** Why it is slow: none of these are broken; they carry only 14–25 requests and modest data, so the cost is almost entirely the fixed per-page overhead plus a tracking call that holds the page for 0.3–0.5 seconds. Suggestion: **move the visitor-tracking call out of the blocking load path**, which should pull this whole group comfortably inside the acceptable band without touching the features.
- **Live Arrivals pre-flight overhead (Admin run).** Why it is slow: two of the five slowest calls are "pre-flight" permission checks that return no data at all yet consume 1.7 seconds each. Suggestion: **have the platform make these permission checks once per session instead of on every view**, removing a delay that produces zero value for the user.

## Recommended next steps

1. **Get the live-arrivals monitor below 4 seconds for every user.** Consolidate the hundreds of per-country lookups into a single feed. This is the highest-value fix in the report: it removes roughly 4–5 seconds of waiting from the screen used in live operational situations, and it does so once for all three roles and both airports.
2. **Make Region Master feel instant by loading it in stages.** Show the region structure in under 3 seconds and pull country detail only when someone selects a country. This removes the second-longest wait in the platform and makes the screen usable for routine reference lookups.
3. **Remove the fixed delay that every single page pays.** Have the shared layout file downloaded once instead of twice, and stop the visitor-tracking call from holding up the page. Even the "acceptable" screens would improve by roughly 0.5–1 second each, and the change costs little because it touches shared behaviour rather than features.
4. **Shrink the data the platform ships on first view.** The live-arrivals and region screens send around 1 megabyte of request data each; asking for less up front, and loading the rest only when needed, reduces the load on our own infrastructure and makes the product work on the weaker connections used in airport operations rooms.
5. **Adopt a "4-second" rule and keep watching it.** Set the standard that no screen a user waits on may exceed 4 seconds, put the existing measurement into the regular build check so slowdowns are caught before release, and re-measure monthly across all three roles. Without this, improvements tend to be quietly undone by new features.
